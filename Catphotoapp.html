Feline Psychology

What Do Cats Love & Hate?

Understanding your cat's preferences is the first step to a happy relationship.

💚 Things Cats Love

  • 🧶 Playing with toys, strings & feather wands
  • 😴 Warm, cosy napping spots in sunbeams
  • 🐭 Hunting games — real or simulated
  • 💆 Gentle head scratches and slow blinks
  • 🪟 Window perches for bird watching
  • 🎵 Calm environments with soft sounds
  • 📦 Cardboard boxes — always free!
  • 🐾 Consistent daily routines

💔 Things Cats Hate

  • 💊 Medicine and unexpected vet visits
  • ⛈️ Loud noises, thunderstorms, fireworks
  • 🚿 Surprise baths when they're dry
  • 🏃 Sudden changes to their daily routine
  • 👃 Strong smells — citrus, essential oils
  • 😤 Being stared at by unfamiliar faces
  • 📲 Loud, sudden notification sounds
  • 🤚 Belly touches they didn't invite

Fascinating Feline Facts

🔊

Cats can produce over 100 different vocalizations — far more than dogs.

👁️

Cats have a third eyelid — the nictitating membrane — for extra eye protection.

🏃

Cats can sprint up to 30 mph (48 km/h) in short bursts of pure energy.

😴

Cats sleep 12–16 hours a day — that's 70% of their entire lives!

👃

A cat's sense of smell is 14 times more powerful than a human's.

👂

Cats can rotate their ears independently up to 180 degrees.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Consider your activity level, living space, time availability, and grooming tolerance. Our interactive Breed Finder Quiz takes just 2 minutes and gives you a personalised recommendation. High-energy breeds like Bengals need lots of play, while calm breeds like Persians suit quieter homes.

Most cats need brushing 2–3 times per week to prevent matting and reduce shedding. Long-haired breeds like Persians and Maine Coons require daily grooming. Short-haired breeds like Abyssinians and Russian Blues are largely self-sufficient.

Cats are obligate carnivores and need high-quality animal protein. Choose premium commercial cat food with a named meat as the first ingredient. Avoid toxic foods like onions, garlic, chocolate, grapes, and xylitol. A mix of wet and dry food supports hydration and dental health.

Start by confining them to one quiet room with food, water, a litter box, and familiar scents. Gradually introduce them to new spaces over 1–2 weeks. Provide hiding spots and maintain calm routines. Patience is key — adjustment can take anywhere from days to several weeks.

Kittens need more frequent visits in their first year for vaccinations. Adult cats (1–7 years) need annual check-ups. Senior cats (7+ years) benefit from biannual visits to catch age-related conditions early. Preventive care is always less expensive than treatment.

Watch for changes in eating, drinking, litter habits, behaviour, or coat condition. Signs include vomiting (more than occasionally), diarrhea, lethargy, weight loss, excessive grooming, or breathing difficulties. When in doubt, always consult your vet — early intervention saves lives.
