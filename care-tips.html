Cat Care Tips

Everything you need to keep your feline companion healthy, happy, and thriving — organised by topic.

🥩 Quality Diet

  • Feed premium cat food formulated for your cat's life stage (kitten, adult, senior)
  • Look for named meat (chicken, salmon) as the first ingredient
  • Avoid artificial preservatives, colours, and excessive fillers
  • Mix wet and dry food for hydration balance and dental benefits
  • Always provide fresh, clean water in a ceramic or stainless bowl

⏰ Feeding Schedule

  • Kittens (under 1 year): Feed 3–4 small meals per day
  • Adult cats: Feed once or twice daily at consistent times
  • Senior cats (10+ years): Smaller, more frequent meals
  • Measure portions carefully — obesity is common in indoor cats
  • Consider puzzle feeders to slow fast eaters and provide stimulation

☠️ Dangerous Foods to Avoid

  • Chocolate & caffeine — toxic to the nervous system
  • Onions, garlic, and chives — damage red blood cells
  • Grapes and raisins — can cause acute kidney failure
  • Xylitol (artificial sweetener) — found in gum, yoghurt, baked goods
  • Raw dough, alcohol, and macadamia nuts
  • Cooked bones — splinter dangerously

💧 Hydration

  • Many cats are chronically under-hydrated — wet food helps significantly
  • Water fountains encourage drinking through running water
  • Place multiple water stations away from food bowls
  • Change water daily and clean bowls at least twice a week
  • Signs of dehydration: skin tent test, dry gums, lethargy

🖌️ Coat Care

  • Brush long-haired cats (Persian, Maine Coon) daily to prevent tangles
  • Short-haired cats need brushing 2–3 times per week
  • Use a slicker brush for detangling and a fine-tooth comb for finishing
  • Start grooming routines early in kittenhood for best acceptance
  • Check for fleas, ticks, or skin irritation during every session

✂️ Nails, Ears & Teeth

  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks using proper cat nail clippers
  • Only clip the clear tip — avoid the pink quick
  • Clean ears weekly with a vet-approved solution and cotton ball
  • Never insert anything into the ear canal
  • Brush teeth 2–3 times per week with cat-specific toothpaste
  • Dental treats and water additives can supplement brushing

🚿 Bathing

  • Most cats groom themselves and rarely need baths
  • Sphynx cats require weekly warm-water baths for skin care
  • If needed, use a cat-specific mild shampoo — never human products
  • Keep water lukewarm and work quickly to minimise stress
  • Thoroughly dry with a warm towel or low-heat dryer setting

🏡 Litter Box Hygiene

  • Scoop waste at least once daily — cats refuse dirty boxes
  • Replace all litter completely every 1–2 weeks
  • Provide 1 litter box per cat, plus one extra
  • Keep boxes in quiet, accessible locations away from food
  • Wash the box itself with mild unscented soap monthly

🏥 Preventive Care

  • Kittens: vet visit at 8, 12, and 16 weeks for vaccinations
  • Adult cats: annual wellness check-ups
  • Senior cats (7+ years): biannual appointments
  • Spay/neuter by 5–6 months — reduces health risks significantly
  • Keep vaccinations, flea, tick, and worming treatments current

🚨 Warning Signs

  • Sudden changes in eating, drinking, or litter box habits
  • Vomiting more than 2–3 times per week
  • Unexplained weight loss or bloating
  • Laboured breathing or persistent coughing
  • Excessive grooming leading to bald patches
  • Any difficulty urinating — this is an emergency

🦷 Dental Health

  • 80% of cats show signs of dental disease by age 3
  • Regular brushing is the gold standard — start as a kitten
  • Dry food alone does not clean teeth effectively
  • VOHC-approved dental treats and water additives help
  • Annual professional dental cleanings are recommended

🧬 Parasite Control

  • Treat for fleas year-round, especially in warm climates
  • Use vet-recommended treatments — avoid over-the-counter pyrethrins
  • Deworm kittens every 2 weeks until 12 weeks, then monthly until 6 months
  • Indoor cats still need regular parasite prevention
  • Check for ear mites if your cat scratches ears frequently

🏠 Safe Living Space

  • Secure all windows and balcony gaps — cats are escape artists
  • Remove toxic plants: lilies, pothos, philodendron, oleander
  • Store chemicals, medications, and small objects safely out of reach
  • Provide multiple vertical spaces — cats feel safer when elevated
  • Keep washing machines and dryers closed before use

🧸 Enrichment & Play

  • Rotate toys weekly to maintain novelty and prevent boredom
  • Dedicate 15–20 minutes of interactive play twice daily
  • Feather wands, laser pointers, and crinkle balls are favourites
  • Cat trees, shelves, and window perches satisfy climbing instincts
  • Food puzzles and slow feeders provide mental stimulation

🌿 Indoor vs Outdoor

  • Indoor cats live significantly longer (12–18 vs 5–7 years on average)
  • If outdoor access is desired, catio enclosures are the safest option
  • Leash training is possible — start young for best results
  • Microchip and collar-tag all cats, even predominantly indoor ones
  • Ensure outdoor cats are vaccinated for additional disease exposure

🛏️ Rest & Comfort

  • Provide multiple sleeping spots — cats like options and rotation
  • Heated cat beds are especially loved by senior cats
  • Ensure hiding spots exist — cats need private retreat spaces
  • Maintain consistent ambient temperature (18–25°C / 65–77°F)
  • Avoid sudden loud music, shouting, or appliance noise near rest areas

🧩 Understanding Body Language

  • Slow blink = trust and affection — blink back to communicate love
  • Tail straight up = greeting, contentment, confidence
  • Flattened ears = fear, agitation, or aggression — give space
  • Exposed belly = comfort, but not always an invitation to touch
  • Chirping at windows = hunting instinct triggered by birds or prey

🏠 New Cat Introductions

  • Confine the new cat to one room for 1–2 weeks initially
  • Swap bedding between cats to exchange scents before meeting
  • Feed cats on opposite sides of a closed door to build positive association
  • First visual contact through a baby gate before full introduction
  • Expect 2–4 weeks for full adjustment — patience is everything

😤 Scratching & Spraying

  • Provide multiple scratching posts — tall sisal posts are preferred
  • Place posts near sleeping areas and high-traffic zones
  • Never punish scratching — redirect to appropriate surfaces
  • Spraying often indicates stress, a dirty litter box, or medical issues
  • Feliway (synthetic pheromone) diffusers can reduce stress behaviours

😺 Positive Reinforcement

  • Cats respond to reward, not punishment — always use positive reinforcement
  • High-value treats (tiny pieces of chicken or tuna) are powerful motivators
  • Clicker training is highly effective even for adult cats
  • Keep training sessions short (3–5 minutes) and end on a success
  • Ignore unwanted behaviours rather than scolding when safe to do so
